\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

This paper combined size-agnostic set policies with a feasibility-preserving safety layer for V2G charging control at a CPO station.
The layer projects raw actions onto taper-aware per-EV bounds and the two-sided transformer limit in $O(N\log N)$ time, during training and deployment.
Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} gives a two-layer result.
Under the one-step model and the grid-headroom condition, the transformer limit holds at every step for every sequence of raw actions, and every EV whose stay contains no infeasible step meets its admitted departure requirement.

In EV2Gym, no transformer overload occurred in any episode run through the safety layer.
For every non-conformal method with the known-departure bound, no EV was left short in an episode without infeasible steps.
Attention and Deep Sets policies trained at 10 and 20 chargers transferred to 200 chargers.
The attention policy stayed within 0.04\,\texteuro{} per charger and episode of a policy trained at 50 chargers in the point estimates.
As an architecture, attention showed no advantage over Deep Sets.
A price-threshold rule with the same layer had a lower mean gap than the attention policy at every station size of the test grid and than the Deep Sets policy below 20 chargers.

Learned policies caused infeasible steps, and 7.4\% to 34.1\% of the admitted EVs of the attention policy had one in their stay, depending on station size.
With the departure bound, the attention policy left more EVs unserved than a variant without the bound and than its penalty-trained twin.
In penalised profit, it scored higher than this twin, with an inconclusive difference at 200 chargers.

For a CPO, three limitations matter most.
First, the departure result needs declared requirements and departure times, or a calibrated departure bound with marginal coverage only.
Second, the transformer result assumes that each EV draws the commanded power, and real battery management systems may draw less.
Third, the evidence comes from simulation with two training seeds per learned method and lossless economics.
Future work can add a capacity-aware lower bound that reduces infeasible steps, measured-energy feedback for hardware and several transformers with linearised voltage limits.
Tests with recorded charging sessions would narrow the remaining gap between the model and practice.
