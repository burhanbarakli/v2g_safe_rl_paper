## 2.1 Reinforcement learning for EV smart charging and V2G

Deep reinforcement learning (RL) for EV charging has moved from single-vehicle agents to stations and fleets. Li et al. framed the charging of one EV under random arrivals, departures and prices as a constrained Markov decision process [li2020constrained]. Later studies control stations with photovoltaics, storage and V2G [korkas2024distributed; zhang2025dumes], coordinate several stations [song2025longterm], and manage fleets under distribution-grid limits [zhang2024hamlet; caballe2026largescale]. Two reviews list safety, dependence on simulation and the lack of a standard benchmark among the open problems of the field [qiu2023reinforcement; michailidis2025reinforcement]. In most of these papers, the energy owed to a departing vehicle enters the reward as a penalty [sadeghianpourhamami2020definition; korkas2024distributed; ye2022learning].

Among open simulators, EV2Gym models a charge point operator whose chargers sit under transformers with inflexible load, solar generation and demand-response events [orfanoudakis2025ev2gym]. Its default RL interface assigns one action to each charger port and uses a flat observation, so a trained policy is tied to one station size. The simulator records transformer overload without preventing it, so RL agents meet the limit only through a reward penalty. In its V2G benchmark, penalty-based agents earned high profit but satisfied few users, whereas the perfect-information solution served every vehicle. Chargax reimplements a charging station in JAX and reports large speedups over earlier environments [ponse2025chargax]. Its currents are rescaled to respect the station's electrical tree, unmet demand is penalised, and the observation again grows with the number of chargers.

The adaptive scheduling algorithm of ACN solves a convex program in receding horizon on a real workplace network and reports results as a fraction of the offline optimum [lee2021adaptive]. Economic MPC formulations for G2V and V2G with hard departure and transformer constraints are available inside EV2Gym [diazlondono2026open]. Their evaluation compares MPC variants with heuristics, not with a perfect-information optimum. Learning has also been coupled with predictive control through a learned aggregate flexibility signal [li2021learning]. Recent EV2Gym-based work extends the setting to distribution networks and trains actors through a differentiable power-flow model [orfanoudakis2026physics]. Voltage and departure targets remain soft penalties there.

## 2.2 Constraint handling and safe RL

The simplest treatment of constraints adds weighted violation terms to the reward. Power-system studies show that agents trained this way still break limits at test time [tabas2022computationally; fan2024safety]. The constrained MDP replaces the fixed weight with a bound on expected cumulative cost [altman2021constrained]. Lagrangian schemes [tessler2018reward] and trust-region updates [achiam2017constrained] solve it, and recent reviews classify both as soft or probabilistic guarantees [gu2024review; su2025review]. In an EV case, SAC with a Lagrange multiplier lowered voltage violations at charging stations without removing them, and some demand stayed unserved [fan2024safety]. CMDP formulations also appear for transformer thermal stress, solved by projection-based policy optimisation [zhang2025modelfree], and for V2G aggregators, solved by constrained variational policy optimisation [eland2026safe]. The first reports more delivered energy than its benchmarks, and the second reports fewer violations than standard RL baselines.

A second family corrects each proposed action before execution, for example with shields that are synthesised from temporal-logic specifications and override only unsafe actions [alshiekh2018safe]. Safety layers compute a per-state correction from a learned linear constraint model [dalal2018safe] or from a quadratic program with a fallback controller [ceusters2023adaptive]. In EV charging, examples include rule-based local and global shields [guan2024rule], decision-level layers that replace unsafe actions at a renewable-supplied station [zhang2025dumes], and grid-code shielding that still leaves frequent transformer overloads [demirel2025multiobjective]. A filter is only as safe as its internal model. HetGPS combines a learned risk model with a linearised power-flow projection and lowers voltage violations, yet its authors make no pointwise feasibility claim [wang2026hetgps]. GRAS-AVC screens actions with an AC power flow and states that the screen is not a multi-step certificate [jin2026telemetry]. The EV2Gym authors have also added a safety layer to a graph-enhanced TD3 for large-scale dispatch, and its abstract states that the layer should preserve charging requirements and enforce power limits [orfanoudakis2026can]. The strongest family makes the policy output feasible by construction, and differentiable quadratic and convex layers let gradients pass through an exact solve [amos2017optnet; agrawal2019differentiable]. Chen et al. placed such a projection inside energy-system policies and obtained zero voltage violations for inverter control, although each projection call took longer than the policy network itself [chen2021enforcing]. DC3 completes equality constraints and corrects inequalities with unrolled gradient steps [donti2021dc3]. Gauge maps produce feasible points of a polytope in closed form [li2023learning]. Tabas and Zhang used one to prove that a learned frequency-regulation policy stays inside a controlled-invariant safe set [tabas2022computationally].

In EV charging, LIRL projects latent actions onto a logic-defined admissible set with a per-step quadratic program and proves feasibility whenever that set is non-empty [wan2025logicinformed]. Its EV-station case covers transformer and charger-current limits but not departure energy. A depot-charging preprint applies a closed-form projection to a network constraint and reports zero breaches, with no proof stated in its abstract [arif2026constraint]. HPC-RL derives per-EV power bounds from remaining energy and remaining time and couples them with power-flow projection for V2G [yan2026hierarchical]. In the version we read, its formal result covers only first-order power-balance feasibility.

Projection also alters learning, as Gros et al. showed: naive projection biases policy gradients, and they derived corrections for deterministic and stochastic policies [gros2020safe]. Kasaura et al. benchmarked action-constrained RL and described a zero-gradient problem: many infeasible actions map to the same projected point [kasaura2023benchmarking]. In their tests, a critic trained on pre-projection actions with a small penalty was a strong and simple baseline. Markgraf et al. compared projection inside the environment with projection as a policy layer [markgraf2025safe]. Their analysis finds the two equivalent for stochastic policies with generalised advantage estimation and traces rank-deficient Jacobians to action aliasing. A distance penalty between pre- and post-projection actions is a common remedy [chen2021enforcing; markgraf2025safe]. When the feasible set is a box intersected with one sum constraint, the Euclidean projection is a continuous quadratic knapsack problem with exact breakpoint and semismooth-Newton solvers [kiwiel2007breakpoint; wang2022efficient].

## 2.3 Scalable and size-agnostic architectures

Set-based architectures offer one route to policies that accept any number of units, and Deep Sets characterises permutation-invariant functions as sum-decomposable [zaheer2017deep]. Self-attention [vaswani2017attention] and the Set Transformer [lee2019set] model interactions among set elements with shared weights, and inducing points make attention cost linear in set size. In RL, attention over node tokens produced strong routing policies [kool2019attention]. UPDeT decoded entity-wise actions from a transformer so that one model served multi-agent tasks of different sizes [hu2021updet]. Mean-field RL takes a different path and replaces neighbours by their average action [yang2018mean]. Size transfer is not automatic, since neural TSP solvers trained on small graphs often fail on larger instances [joshi2022learning]. Re-attending to the remaining nodes at each step helped one model generalise to 1000 nodes [luo2023neural].

In EV charging, size independence first came from aggregation, as when Sadeghianpourhamami et al. binned connected vehicles by time to departure and remaining charging time [sadeghianpourhamami2020definition]. The resulting state has the same size for any number of stations. On 10 and 50 stations, their policy cost 13% and 15.6% more than a perfect-knowledge optimum. A policy trained on 10 stations was also evaluated with up to tenfold arrivals. Aggregator-level agents dispatch one scalar power to vehicles by rules [zhang2022transfer], and a VPP agent acts on aggregate flexibility within bounds meant to ensure individual charging requirements [salesortiz2024efficient]. Grouping vehicles with equal remaining time [zhang2024hamlet] and mean-field games [caballe2026largescale] follow similar logic. Such representations transfer across sizes but hide per-vehicle detail or move it into disaggregation rules.

A second route shares parameters across chargers or vehicles: one DQN can serve all chargers of a station [ye2022learning], and per-spot DDPG agents can keep fixed local observations [korkas2024distributed]. Shared per-vehicle policies have also transferred zero-shot to unseen cities [valesalonso2026emergent]. In the recent EV2Gym line, EV-GNN builds a heterogeneous graph of vehicles, chargers, transformers and the operator and scales to 1000 charge points [orfanoudakis2025scalable]. Its reward penalises transformer overload and unmet user demand with heavy weights, and the authors note that classic RL methods often lack constraint satisfaction guarantees. GNN-DT pairs graph embeddings with an offline decision transformer and transfers zero-shot from 25 to 100 chargers, with performance degrading as size grows [orfanoudakis2026graph]. HetGPS keeps a single parameter set from 1,236 to 3,218 vehicles [wang2026hetgps]. Transformer-based multi-agent RL has also been applied to collaborative charging across several stations [song2025longterm].

In power-system control, T-MAAC treats buses as tokens of a transformer policy but trains each network separately [wang2022stabilizing]. GRAS-AVC deploys one graph actor, trained on feeders of up to 141 buses, on a 322-bus feeder without retraining [jin2026telemetry]. Across the size-agnostic EV and grid controllers we could read in full, constraints enter through reward terms, imitation data or heuristic screens. None of them enforces departure energy and the transformer limit by construction.

## 2.4 Laxity-based scheduling and departure uncertainty

Laxity measures the slack between the time left before departure and the time still needed to finish charging. Chen et al. proved that a schedule is feasible exactly when every laxity stays non-negative [chen2022smoothed]. Their smoothed least-laxity-first rule clips each rate between zero and its cap around a common level, and bisection on that level matches the station power limit. On real traces, sLLF reached full feasibility with little extra power, and the authors proved resource-augmentation bounds for online operation. Deadline and latest-start priorities also perform well under hard cable capacities [vanhuffelen2025grid]. LLF serves as a dispatch layer below learned aggregate decisions [li2021learning], and rule-based aggregators move high-laxity vehicles out of overload periods [christensen2025multiagent]. The per-EV lower bound of HPC-RL applies latest-start reasoning of the same kind [yan2026hierarchical].

Most of these methods assume that each user declares departure time and requested energy at arrival, and evidence suggests the assumption is fragile. For domestic chargers, Dyo found plug-in durations nearly unpredictable from history, with percentage errors above 400% [dyo2022behavior]. At workplaces, regression forecasts of departure improved prioritisation under scarce supply [frendo2021improving]. A fluid-limit analysis of EDF and LLF shows that users gain by under-reporting deadlines unless the scheduler stops at the declared time [ferragut2022scheduling]. In an imbalance-market study, PPO policies trained without departure uncertainty outperformed those trained with it [naghdizadeganjahromi2026adaptive]. Our screening found no peer-reviewed work that turns departure forecasts into calibrated, distribution-free bounds for an RL safety layer.
