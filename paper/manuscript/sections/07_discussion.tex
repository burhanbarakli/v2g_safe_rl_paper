\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:limitations}

The departure result of Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} rests on declared departure times and requirements (A2).
Our conformal extension relaxes only the departure step, its coverage is marginal, and strategic misreporting of deadlines \cite{ferragut2022scheduling} lies outside the model.
Aggregate feasibility (A5) can fail in closed loop, as the two-EV example of \Sec{sec:invariance} shows.
On such steps the departure requirement is guaranteed only for the EVs covered by Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance}(b), and Lemma~\ref{S-lem:deficit} bounds the remaining shortfall without removing it.
A layer that certified joint feasibility over the future would need time-coupled constraints and would lose the single-sum projection.

The transformer result needs the one-step model A3 and the exogenous headroom of A6, and the controller does not enforce A6.
Lemma~\ref{S-lem:a3} proves A3 for the EV2Gym settings we use, whereas a real battery management system may draw less than commanded.
Under-delivery lowers the energy of an EV below the value the bounds assume, so hardware would need a margin on the thresholds or feedback from measured energy, a remedy not analysed here.
A1 holds for EV2Gym's exponential taper, but a much steeper taper would break it.
The model covers one station behind one transformer without voltage limits or line flows, and further coupled sum constraints would fall outside Proposition~\ref{prop:projection}.

The experiments use EV2Gym's lossless accounting, and the discharge price equals the charging price.
V2G arbitrage therefore carries no round-trip loss and no degradation cost, so profits from discharging may be optimistic, and the sensitivities probe this effect only at one station size.
All results come from simulation with synthetic arrivals from EV2Gym's statistics and Dutch day-ahead prices, some late evening sessions do not fit into the episode horizon, and forecast noise is synthetic.
Grid quantities scale linearly with $N$, so the per-charger statistics of the test stations match training, and only the stress level changes them.

Some comparisons are not fully like-for-like.
EV-GNN trains per profile at one size with its own algorithm and reward, on one load and PV draw per run and with fewer steps than its default.
Our eMPC-HiGHS re-implementation knows the exact prices and arrivals inside its horizon, the MLP is reported only at $N\le20$, and GNN-DT was not run.
The optima use outer taper rows, exact except on a one-step transition band and a 1\% margin, together with the rounding allowance.
They also ignore the information limits of an online controller, so part of every gap is the price of not knowing the future.
