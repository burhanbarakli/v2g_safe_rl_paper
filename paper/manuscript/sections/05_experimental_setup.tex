\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Simulator and scenarios}\label{sec:scenarios}

All experiments use EV2Gym \cite{orfanoudakis2025ev2gym} at commit \texttt{55ef368c} without changes to its source.
A wrapper computes the observations, applies the safety layer, the fallback rule and the actuation map, logs all metrics and never reads future arrivals.
Each station has $N$ chargers with one port each behind a single transformer, and EV specifications are drawn from the eight V2G-capable EV models of the simulator with one constant efficiency per model.
The desired energy is the full battery, prices are the Dutch day-ahead prices shipped with EV2Gym, and the discharge price equals the charging price.
\Tab{S-tab:scenarios} in the supplementary material lists all scenario parameters, and Section~\ref{S-sec:ev2gym} documents the modelling details.

Every exogenous quantity scales linearly with $N$.
The transformer rating is $P_{\sm{tr}}=\rho_{\sm{tr}}N$ with a tight level of $\rho_{\sm{tr}}=4$ kW per charger, the ratio of EV2Gym's 25-charger V2G configuration, and a moderate level of 7 kW.
Inflexible load and PV are scaled to a mean peak of $0.4P_{\sm{tr}}$, so the PV peak stays below the rating.
Forecasts in the observation carry Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 10\% of the value.
Three arrival profiles of EV2Gym are used, namely workplace, public and private (residential).
Their start hours and spawn multipliers are chosen so that overnight sessions fit into the 28-h horizon and the mean charger occupancy is about 0.21 for all profiles.
The policy does not observe the profile, so the profile forms a further zero-shot dimension.

Training mixes $N\in\{10,20\}$, the three profiles and the two ratings within every batch.
The test grid covers $N\in\{5,10,20,50,100,200\}$, both ratings and all profiles, i.e.\ 36 configurations with 100 fixed test episodes each for $N\le50$ and 50 for $N\ge100$.
EV2Gym's original 25-charger configuration adds 100 episodes for comparability with earlier EV2Gym studies, so each evaluated policy runs 3,100 test episodes shared across methods.
Two sensitivity studies at $N=20$ and $\rho_{\sm{tr}}=4$ kW cover all three profiles.
The first evaluates the main policies with a desired state of charge of 0.8, and the second trains a separate policy with $\Delta t=5$ min and $\gamma=0.99^{1/3}$.

\subsection{Compared methods}\label{sec:baselines}

The learned baselines isolate the two parts of \method{}: Attention+penalty uses the same attention policy without the safety layer and learns from a penalty reward.
A multilayer perceptron (MLP) with two hidden layers of 256 units over the concatenated tokens, zero-padded to 200 slots, replaces the attention policy in MLP+safety layer and in MLP+penalty.
Without the safety layer, a raw action passes only the per-EV physical clip $\clip(x_i,\pmn_i,u_i)$, and that clip contains neither the lower bound $l_i$ nor the transformer coupling.
The penalty reward subtracts 10\,\texteuro{} per kWh of departure shortfall and per kWh of transformer overload energy, more than eleven times the highest hourly price in the data, so no violation can pay.


Four heuristics complete the comparison: as fast as possible (AFAP), as late as possible (ALAP), round robin and LLF, each in a raw form and with its output passed through the safety layer.
The economic MPC of EV2Gym \cite{diazlondono2026open} runs with true prices and arrivals inside its 10-step horizon, an information advantage that is flagged in the results.
Its commercial solver licence limits it to $N\le25$, so larger stations use a related but not identical HiGHS port, reported under its own name.
EV-GNN \cite{orfanoudakis2025scalable} runs from its public code at commit \texttt{01aa1b7a} with its own TD3 training script, reward and published hyperparameters.
Its script accepts one scenario per run, so one model per arrival profile is trained at $N=20$ and $\rho_{\sm{tr}}=4$ kW with five seeds and evaluated zero-shot on all sizes and ratings of that profile.
GNN-DT \cite{orfanoudakis2026graph} is not included, because we found no public code for it.
The perfect-information LP of \Sec{sec:oracle} provides the reference profit $J^{\sm{LP,ceil}}$.

Ablations of \method{} remove admission control, set $\beta=0$, train with a curriculum or use a smaller network.
The conformal variant \method{}-CP($\alpha$) is evaluated at all four levels of $\alpha$.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

Per episode we record the profit in \texteuro{} and the optimality gap of \Eq{eq:gap}.
Service is measured by the departure shortfall in kWh and the share of EVs that miss $\Ereq_i$ by more than 0.01 kWh, and grid safety by the transformer violation energy in kWh.
For the safety layer we count the infeasible steps per fallback case and record the deficit bound of \Eq{eq:deficit} and the number of capped and admission-reduced requests.
The cost of the guarantee is the profit of Attention+penalty minus the profit of \method{} on the same episodes, reported together with the violations that the penalty method incurs.
For the conformal variant, coverage is the episode-averaged fraction of EVs with $d_i\ge\hat d_i^{\sm{lo}}$.

\subsection{Training, tuning and statistics}\label{sec:hyper}

All learned methods of our own code receive the same tuning budget of 18 configurations over learning rate, minibatch size and entropy coefficient, each trained for $0.5\times10^{6}$ steps on the tight 10-charger station.
The selected configuration is retrained with five seeds for $3\times10^{6}$ steps, and variants such as \method{}-CP inherit the selection of their parent method.
\Tab{S-tab:hyper} in the supplementary material lists all hyperparameters and the tuning grid.
In total, the main experiments, ablations and penalty-weight sensitivities comprise 51 training runs of our own PPO code, plus the tuning runs and the optional EV-GNN and 5-min runs.

Aggregate scores over seeds and episodes are reported as the mean and the interquartile mean with stratified-bootstrap 95\% confidence intervals \cite{agarwal2021deep}, following the reproducibility guidance of \cite{henderson2018deep}.
Paired comparisons on common episodes use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test \cite{wilcoxon1945individual} on per-episode profit and shortfall, with Holm correction across $N$.
The implementation uses PyTorch \cite{paszke2019pytorch}, a single-file PPO in the style of CleanRL \cite{huang2022cleanrl} and the Adam optimiser \cite{kingma2015adam}, with the safety layer in double precision.
