\begin{abstract}
Charge point operators that offer vehicle-to-grid (V2G) services must keep a station within its transformer limit and deliver the agreed energy to each driver by departure.
Reinforcement learning (RL) controllers usually treat both requirements as reward penalties, and their networks are tied to one station size.
This paper combines a size-agnostic attention policy with a safety layer, an exact projection onto per-vehicle power bounds and the two-sided transformer limit, computed in $O(N\log N)$ time for $N$ chargers.
Each charger is a token, so one set of weights serves any number of chargers.

The lower bound of each electric vehicle (EV) is the least power after which full-power charging still reaches its admitted requirement, and it includes the charging taper.
This admitted requirement is the driver's request capped to what is reachable and admissible.

For any sequence of raw actions, during training and deployment, the paper proves two results.
Under simulator and grid-headroom conditions, the transformer limit holds at every step.
In addition, every EV whose stay contains no infeasible step meets its admitted requirement, where a step is infeasible if the transformer cannot give every EV its lower bound.
A least-laxity-first fallback rule handles the remaining steps.

For undeclared departures, a split-conformal bound on the departure time extends the second result with marginal coverage of at least the nominal level minus the rate of EVs that receive less than their bound.
The safe attention-based controller (\method{}) is trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO) on stations with 10 and 20 chargers in the EV2Gym simulator and tested without retraining on up to 200 chargers.
It is compared with learned, constrained-RL, heuristic and model-predictive baselines through the gap of a penalised profit to a perfect-information optimum and through the share of EVs without infeasible steps.

\end{abstract}
