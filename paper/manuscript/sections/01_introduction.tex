\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

A charge point operator (CPO) buys energy for its chargers at time-varying prices, and vehicle-to-grid (V2G) discharging lets the same operator sell energy from parked electric vehicles (EVs) when prices are high.
Two hard requirements limit this freedom.
The aggregate power of the station must stay within the transformer limit, and each EV must hold its agreed energy when it leaves.
Otherwise the operator overloads equipment or breaks the service promise to the driver.

Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a common approach to this control task, because prices, arrivals and departures are uncertain and hard to model in advance \cite{qiu2023reinforcement,michailidis2025reinforcement}.
Most RL controllers for EV charging encode the departure energy requirement as a weighted penalty in the reward \cite{sadeghianpourhamami2020definition,ye2022learning,korkas2024distributed,orfanoudakis2025ev2gym}.
Those that model a transformer limit usually penalise overloads in the same way \cite{orfanoudakis2025ev2gym,orfanoudakis2026graph}.
A penalty trades violations against profit, so a trained policy may still violate either requirement at test time \cite{orfanoudakis2025ev2gym,tabas2022computationally}.
Constrained formulations bound the expected cumulative cost instead \cite{altman2021constrained}, but such a bound holds on average over episodes and not at every step \cite{gu2024review}.

A second limitation concerns station size, because a flat observation with one action per charger, as in the default interface of the EV2Gym simulator \cite{orfanoudakis2025ev2gym}, ties a trained network to one number of chargers.
Graph and attention policies remove this restriction \cite{orfanoudakis2025scalable,orfanoudakis2026graph,wang2026hetgps}, yet in these size-agnostic controllers the constraints still enter through reward terms, imitation data or heuristic filters.

Safety layers correct each action before execution and can enforce limits by construction \cite{dalal2018safe,chen2021enforcing,tabas2022computationally}.
For EV stations, existing layers cover per-step limits such as transformer capacity and charger current \cite{wan2025logicinformed}, or they replace unsafe actions \cite{guan2024rule,zhang2025dumes}.
A per-step layer alone does not protect the departure energy requirement, because that requirement couples the present action with every later step of the stay.
Per-EV latest-start bounds address this coupling in laxity-based scheduling \cite{chen2022smoothed} and in a recent hierarchical V2G scheduler \cite{yan2026hierarchical}.
\Sec{sec:rw-position} positions the combination of these elements against the closest works.

This paper studies size-agnostic safe control for V2G charging at a CPO station.
A set policy treats each charger as a token and outputs one raw power action per token with shared weights.
The main instantiation, the safe attention-based controller (\method), uses self-attention, and a Deep Sets policy serves as the alternative set architecture.
A safety layer, an exact projection onto the feasible set, maps the raw actions to commanded powers that respect per-EV bounds and the transformer limit.
The projection onto a box intersected with a two-sided sum constraint is classical \cite{kiwiel2007breakpoint,wang2022efficient}, and the rate rule of smoothed least-laxity-first (sLLF) scheduling has a closely related form \cite{chen2022smoothed}.
Our feasibility argument extends the laxity argument of Chen et al. to the charging taper, V2G discharging and a two-sided coupling.

The paper makes three contributions.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We derive a safety layer whose per-EV lower bound includes the charging taper, and Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} states its result in two layers. The transformer limit holds at every step for every sequence of raw actions under simulator and grid-headroom conditions, and the admitted departure requirement is met by every EV whose stay contains no infeasible step.
\item We train two size-agnostic set policies, an attention policy and a Deep Sets policy, with the layer in the loop on stations with 10 and 20 chargers. Both are tested without retraining on up to 200 chargers, against a reference policy trained at 50 chargers.
\item We report a measured evaluation in EV2Gym of the transformer limit with and without the layer and of the conditional departure result at episode level. It also measures how often learned policies cause infeasible steps, the cost of the guarantee, simple rules with the same layer and a split-conformal departure bound.
\end{enumerate}
The results do not show an advantage of the attention policy over the Deep Sets policy.
A price-threshold rule with the safety layer had a lower mean gap than the attention policy at every station size of the test grid.
Only the Deep Sets policy beat this rule, and only from 20 chargers upward.
For the attention policy, the departure bound also came with more unserved EVs than a variant without it.
None of the ingredients is new in isolation, so the claim of the paper rests on their combination, on the two-layer result and on its measured behaviour.
