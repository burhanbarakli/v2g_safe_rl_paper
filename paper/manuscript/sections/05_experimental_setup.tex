\section{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Simulator and scenarios}\label{sec:scenarios}

All experiments use EV2Gym \cite{orfanoudakis2025ev2gym} at commit \texttt{55ef368c} without changes to its source.
A wrapper computes the observations, applies the shared admitted requirements, the safety layer, the fallback rule and the actuation, and logs all metrics without reading future arrivals.
Each station has $N$ chargers behind one transformer, and EVs are drawn from the eight V2G-capable EV models of the simulator with one constant efficiency per model (0.90, and 0.93 for one model).
The desired energy is the full battery, prices are the Dutch day-ahead prices shipped with EV2Gym, and the discharge price equals the charging price.
Table~\ref{S-tab:scenarios} lists all scenario parameters.

The grid quantities and the arrival rate scale linearly with $N$.
In particular, the transformer rating is $P_{\sm{tr}}=\rho_{\sm{tr}}N$, with a tight level of $\rho_{\sm{tr}}=4$ kW per charger as in EV2Gym's 25-charger V2G configuration and a moderate level of 7 kW.
Inflexible load and PV are scaled with multipliers of mean $0.4P_{\sm{tr}}$, and forecasts in the observation carry Gaussian noise with a standard deviation of 10\%.
The workplace, public and private arrival profiles of EV2Gym start at hours that let overnight sessions fit into the 28-h horizon of $T=112$ steps of 15 min.

Training mixes $N\in\{10,20\}$, the three profiles and the two ratings within every batch.
The test grid covers $N\in\{5,10,20,50,100,200\}$, both ratings and all profiles, plus a stress level of $\rho_{\sm{tr}}=3$ kW at $N\in\{20,200\}$ and EV2Gym's original 25-charger configuration, 43 configurations in total.
Zero-shot therefore refers to the station size and to the stress level, whereas the profiles are seen in training.
Each configuration has 100 fixed test episodes for $N\le50$ and 50 for $N\ge100$, shared by all methods, so each policy runs 3,550 test episodes.
The stress level is the only sensitivity study that was run.

\subsection{Compared methods}\label{sec:baselines}

Every method and every evaluation pass receives the same admitted requirements, and shortfall is always measured against them.
Methods without the layer execute only the physical clip $\clip(x_i,\pmn_i,u_i)$ without $l_i$ or the transformer coupling.

Among the learned methods, \method{} and Deep Sets+safety layer are the two size-agnostic set policies with the safety layer.
Attention+penalty is the penalty-based twin of \method{}: it learns from the profit minus a weight $w$ times the shortfall and overload energy, without the safety layer.
Its constrained counterpart, Attention+PPO-Lagrangian, replaces the fixed weight by Lagrange multipliers with a cost limit of zero \cite{altman2021constrained,tessler2018reward}.
With $l_i=\pmn_i$, Attention+safety layer without departure bound reduces the layer to a per-step transformer projection as in \cite{wan2025logicinformed} and learns the departure requirement from the penalty reward.
The penalty-trained checkpoints also run with the safety layer at deployment only, and \method{} and Attention+penalty also run without admission control, keeping the arrival-capped request.
A multilayer perceptron (MLP) over tokens zero-padded to 20 slots, with and without the layer, is a fixed-size reference evaluated only at $N\le20$.
As an in-distribution reference, \method{} is also trained at $N=50$ and evaluated at $N\in\{50,100,200\}$.

Six rules complete the comparison, each with and without the safety layer: as fast as possible (AFAP), as late as possible (ALAP), round robin, LLF, sLLF \cite{chen2022smoothed} and a price-threshold rule.
Our HiGHS re-implementation of the economic MPC of EV2Gym \cite{diazlondono2026open}, eMPC-HiGHS, runs at every $N$ with a 10-step horizon and knows the exact prices and arrivals inside it.
The conformal variant \method{}-CP is trained once at $\alpha=0.1$ and evaluated with bounds recalibrated for every $\alpha\in\{0.01,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$, with an uncalibrated point prediction as a further pass.
EV-GNN \cite{orfanoudakis2025scalable} was planned as an external baseline but was not run, and GNN-DT \cite{orfanoudakis2026graph} was not ported, because it trains offline on datasets that an optimiser generates for each scenario.
The planned ablations, penalty-weight sensitivities, further MPC variants and other sensitivity studies were not run either.

\subsection{Metrics}\label{sec:metrics}

The main metric is the absolute gap of \Eq{eq:gap}, valid for every method.
Penalised profits use energy not served against the shared admitted requirement for every method and pass, and profit, energy not served and transformer overload energy are reported separately.
For the safety layer we report the share of EVs whose stay contains no infeasible step (E4), the infeasible steps per fallback case and the measured shortfall against the deficit bound of Lemma~\ref{S-lem:deficit}.
Because the logs carry no per-EV flag for the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance}(b), its departure part is checked at episode level.

The cost of the guarantee is the difference between the penalty-based twin and the policy with the safety layer on the same episodes, in $J^{\sm{pen}}$ as the primary and in profit as the secondary measure.
It is a ratio of sums over matched episodes in \texteuro{} per admitted EV and in percent of $|J^{\sm{LP,ceil}}|$.
A positive value means that the penalty-based twin scored higher.
The transfer gap of \method{} to the reference trained at $N=50$ separates transfer loss from method loss.

\subsection{Tuning, statistics and compute}\label{sec:hyper}

Each of the seven tuned learned methods received 12 configurations from a grid over learning rate, minibatch size and entropy coefficient.
Penalty rewards add the weight $w\in\{1,10,100\}$ to the grid, and the Lagrangian method adds the multiplier step size.
Every configuration was trained on the training mixture for $0.5\times10^{6}$ steps and evaluated on 24 validation episodes disjoint from the test replays, and selection used the validation $J^{\sm{pen}}$ at $w=10$ for every method.
The selected configuration was retrained with two seeds for $3\times10^{6}$ steps, and Table~\ref{S-tab:hyper} lists the hyperparameters.

With two seeds, between-seed variability cannot be tested, so the analysis compares seed-averaged policies on the shared test replays.
Tables report the mean of the seed-averaged policy with the range over the two seeds.
Paired Wilcoxon signed-rank tests \cite{wilcoxon1945individual} on replay-level differences compare \method{} with each other method per station size.
Holm's correction runs over all comparisons of one results table, up to 351 per family, and the sign of the rank-biserial correlation gives the direction.
Confidence intervals of the mean come from a bootstrap that resamples test replays within each configuration, and the interquartile mean (IQM) of the gap is reported at the training sizes \cite{agarwal2021deep}.
The Wilcoxon test concerns the median shift, whereas the interval concerns the mean, and a few episodes with large penalties can move the mean alone.
Where the two disagree, we report both and call the comparison inconclusive.
Results over few seeds can mislead \cite{henderson2018deep}, so the seed count is a limitation (Section~\ref{S-app:stats}).

The implementation uses PyTorch \cite{paszke2019pytorch}, a single-file PPO in the style of CleanRL \cite{huang2022cleanrl} and the Adam optimiser \cite{kingma2015adam}, with the safety layer in double precision.
All experiments ran on an AMD Ryzen 7 7700 with an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 5070 Ti (16 GB) under WSL2 Ubuntu 24.04 with Python 3.13.15 and PyTorch 2.14.1.
Preparation took 1.6 h, tuning 7.9 h, training 10.2 h and evaluation 2.5 h, for 153,100 episodes in total (Section~\ref{S-app:compute}).
