\begin{abstract}
Charge point operators that offer vehicle-to-grid (V2G) services must schedule many electric vehicles (EVs) under a transformer limit and deliver the energy that each driver requests by departure.
Reinforcement learning (RL) controllers for this task usually treat both requirements as reward penalties, and their policy networks are tied to one station size.
This paper proposes a safe attention policy (SA-Attn) that joins a size-agnostic policy with a closed-form safety layer.
Each charger is a token of a transformer encoder, and a shared head maps every token to a raw power action, so one set of weights serves any number of chargers.
The safety layer projects the raw actions onto a feasible set defined by per-EV power bounds and by the forward and reverse transformer limits.
Its lower bound for each EV is the least power that still lets full-power charging reach the departure energy, computed from a reachable-energy operator that includes the charging taper.
The projection reduces to clipping each action around one common shift, found in $O(N\log N)$ time for $N$ chargers.

Under stated assumptions, the paper proves that the transformer limit and every departure energy requirement hold at every step for any raw action, during training and deployment.
A least-laxity-first fallback rule and admission control handle steps on which the assumptions fail, and these steps are counted.
Uncertain departure times are handled by a split-conformal lower bound that turns the guarantee into a coverage-level statement.
The policy is trained with proximal policy optimization (PPO) on stations with 10 and 20 chargers in the EV2Gym simulator.
Without retraining, it is evaluated on stations with up to 200 chargers, three arrival profiles and two transformer ratings.
Performance is reported as the optimality gap to a perfect-information linear program with hard constraints, and the cost of the guarantee as the profit difference to a penalty-trained attention policy.

\end{abstract}
