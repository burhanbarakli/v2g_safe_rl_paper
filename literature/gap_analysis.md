# Gap analysis: closest works versus C1, C2, C3

Literature Agent A1, synthesis step. Date: 2026-10-21.
Inputs: `merged/paper_matrix.csv` (85 verified papers), `merged/references.bib`, `parts/L0-L3_notes.md`, `parts/DONE_L0-L3.md`, and the facts the orchestrator verified today on publisher pages (EV-GNN, eland2026safe, orfanoudakis2026can, song2025longterm).

Reading conventions used below:
- **FT-digest** means the part agent read the "full text" through WebFetch. WebFetch returns a model-written digest, not the verbatim paper, so every number taken from such a reading carries the flag **(n)** and must be checked against the PDF before it goes into the manuscript.
- **ABS** means only the abstract was read. Claims from ABS papers stay at abstract level.
- **ORCH** means the orchestrator read the publisher page in a browser today.
- "?" means unknown. The reason is given in the cell or in the notes under the table.

Contributions under test:
- **C1.** Scale-agnostic transformer-encoder policy (charger = token, shared per-token actor head, masking). One set of weights for any number of chargers.
- **C2.** Closed-form differentiable projection p_i = clip(a_i - lambda, l_i, u_i). The per-EV lower bound l_i is the minimum power now that still lets full-power charging later meet the departure energy. The upper bound is u_i, and aggregate transformer bounds couple the EVs. A feasibility-invariance theorem (by induction) holds under stated assumptions: known departure time and energy, and aggregate feasibility. The layer is used in training and deployment. Least-laxity-first fallback and admission control handle infeasible steps.
- **C3.** Zero-shot transfer from 10-20 chargers to about 200 chargers in EV2Gym, with the optimality gap to the perfect-information optimum and the measured "cost of the guarantee".

## (b) What is already known (ingredients that are NOT new)

Each ingredient of C1-C3 already exists somewhere. The list below is meant to stop the manuscript from claiming any single piece as a first.

1. **Clip around a common threshold.** The form p_i = clip(a_i - lambda, l_i, u_i) with a scalar found by one-dimensional search is the sLLF rate rule [chen2022smoothed]. There the per-EV rate is clipped to [0, min(cap, remaining energy)] around a common level found by bisection under the station power limit. It is also the textbook KKT solution of the Euclidean projection onto a box intersected with one sum constraint. That problem is the continuous quadratic knapsack problem, with linear-time breakpoint algorithms [kiwiel2007breakpoint] and a semismooth-Newton method with an explicit generalised Jacobian for the half-space case [wang2022efficient]. Our layer is therefore a known projection. What differs is its input (a learned per-token action), the lower bound l_i, the V2G sign range and its use as a differentiable layer during training.
2. **Per-EV latest-start lower bound.** HPC-RL computes P_min = max{0, remaining energy - remaining intervals x P_phys} per EV from the known departure time and target [yan2026hierarchical]. That is the same idea as our l_i. The laxity-feasibility equivalence behind it (feasible iff all laxities are non-negative) is Prop. 1 of [chen2022smoothed]. HPC-RL does not couple the bound with a station limit and gives no theorem for it (in the text read), but the bound itself is not ours.
3. **Projection and optimisation layers in RL.** Differentiable QP and convex layers [amos2017optnet; agrawal2019differentiable], completion-and-correction [donti2021dc3], closed-form gauge maps [li2023learning; tabas2022computationally], learned linear safety layers [dalal2018safe] and QP projections with fallback [ceusters2023adaptive] all exist. [chen2021enforcing] already put a differentiable projection inside energy-system RL policies. [tabas2022computationally] already proved a multi-step safety (invariance) guarantee for a closed-form, differentiable layer in a power system. [wan2025logicinformed] already proved per-step feasibility of a projection for transformer and current limits in an EV-station case.
4. **Gradient handling through projection.** The bias of naive projection and its corrections [gros2020safe], the zero-gradient problem [kasaura2023benchmarking], and action aliasing with the equivalence of "projection in the environment" and "projection in the policy" for stochastic policies [markgraf2025safe] are all documented. A pre/post-projection distance loss is an established remedy [chen2021enforcing; markgraf2025safe]. We should adopt and cite one of these treatments, not claim a new one.
5. **Projection layers in EV charging specifically.** A closed-form projection of a network constraint gave zero breaches and made penalty shaping redundant in a depot controller [arif2026constraint] (preprint, abstract only). Rule-based shields [guan2024rule], action-replacement layers [zhang2025dumes] and a GNN-TD3 safety layer meant to preserve charging requirements and power limits [orfanoudakis2026can] also exist. We cannot claim to be the first safety layer for EV charging RL.
6. **Size-agnostic policies for EV charging.** GNN-DT transfers zero-shot from 25 to 100 chargers [orfanoudakis2026graph]. HetGPS keeps one parameter set from 1,236 to 3,218 EVs [wang2026hetgps]. EV-GNN handles up to 1000 charge points with a graph policy [orfanoudakis2025scalable]. Transformer-based MARL across stations exists [song2025longterm]. Entity-token transformers with per-entity action decoding exist in MARL [hu2021updet], and bus-token transformers exist in voltage control [wang2022stabilizing]. C1 by itself is not new. Its value lies in being the vehicle for C2.
7. **Train small, test large, report the gap to an oracle.** [sadeghianpourhamami2020definition] trained on 10 stations, evaluated under up to 10x arrivals, and reported 13-15.6% cost above a perfect-knowledge optimum. Aggregator-level transfer to much larger fleets is also reported [zhang2022transfer] (arXiv only, with internal inconsistencies). Graph actors transfer zero-shot in power systems [jin2026telemetry]. C3 cannot be framed as the first zero-shot or first oracle-gap study. It can be framed as the first such study for a per-charger policy with a hard per-step guarantee in a V2G, price-driven, transformer-limited setting, at about 200 chargers, against a hard-constrained optimum.
8. **Oracle and MPC baselines.** EV2Gym already ships a Gurobi perfect-information oracle and MPC baselines [orfanoudakis2025ev2gym; diazlondono2026open]. ACN reports performance as a fraction of the offline optimum [lee2021adaptive]. One caveat is ours to fix: the v2.0.0 profit-max oracle treats departure energy as a quadratic penalty, so a hard-constrained optimum requires our own modification and must be described as such.
9. **The "cost of safety".** Ablations with and without a projection layer exist [arif2026constraint; kasaura2023benchmarking; markgraf2025safe]. A systematic measurement of how the guarantee's cost scales with N, against a hard-constrained optimum, was not found.

## (c) Novelty statement

> The closest learned controllers, GNN-DT [orfanoudakis2026graph], EV-GNN [orfanoudakis2025scalable] and HetGPS [wang2026hetgps], use size-agnostic graph or attention policies. They handle departure energy and power limits through reward penalties, imitation data or a filter whose authors disclaim pointwise feasibility. The closest constraint mechanisms are not learned policies of variable size. sLLF [chen2022smoothed] applies the same clip-around-a-common-level form as a fixed scheduling rule. HPC-RL [yan2026hierarchical] bounds per-EV power from remaining energy, with no station limit and no proof for that bound. LIRL [wan2025logicinformed] proves per-step feasibility of a solver-based projection but omits departure energy and fixes the action size. Sadeghianpourhamami et al. [sadeghianpourhamami2020definition] already report train-small, test-large results with an oracle gap, using an aggregated state and penalties. Our work joins a charger-token transformer policy with a closed-form differentiable projection used in both training and deployment. Under stated assumptions, we prove that departure energy and the transformer limit stay feasible at every step. We then measure zero-shot optimality gaps up to about 200 chargers against a hard-constrained optimum and quantify the cost of the guarantee. These claims are conditional. They hold only if the full texts of [orfanoudakis2026can], a GNN-enhanced TD3 with a safety layer, and [song2025longterm] do not already prove such a guarantee for a policy that transfers across sizes.

(Word count: about 210.)

Claims to avoid in the manuscript: "first scale-agnostic EV charging policy", "first safety/projection layer for EV charging", "first zero-shot transfer", "first provable constraint satisfaction in EV RL", "novel projection". Use "to our knowledge, the first combination of ..." only after the risk-register checks below are closed.

## (e) Recommendation for G1

**Recommendation: proceed with the main contribution set (C1-C3), reframed as a combination. Add one small secondary contribution: a conformal lower bound on departure time feeding l_i. Make the decision final only after R1 is closed.**

Reasoning:
1. **The combination appears open in everything we could read.** No paper we read combines (i) one set of weights for any number of chargers, (ii) a per-step guarantee covering both departure energy and the transformer limit, proven as an invariant, and (iii) zero-shot gaps to a hard-constrained optimum. Every competitor misses at least two of the three. The scalable EV policies (GNN-DT, EV-GNN, HetGPS) have no hard guarantee, and HetGPS disclaims one explicitly. The hard-constraint works (HPC-RL, LIRL, sLLF) are fixed-size, unproven for departure energy, or not learned.
2. **Each part alone is weak.** C1 is established practice. C2's algebra is known. C3's protocol has a 2020 precedent. The manuscript must therefore sell the invariance theorem inside a learned size-agnostic policy, plus the cost-of-guarantee curve versus N. That is a coherent, modest-scope contribution and fits SAUCIS.
3. **Fallback (a), departure-time uncertainty with a conformal lower bound, is the best secondary addition.** It attacks the main weakness of C2 (the known-departure assumption, R9). It reuses the same layer: replacing d_i by a calibrated lower quantile tightens l_i and turns the deterministic guarantee into a coverage-level statement. The screening found no peer-reviewed conformal-departure work for EV control, but recall was limited (R10), so this novelty must be re-checked. Keep it small: one calibration method, one dataset split, one figure of shortfall and cost versus the coverage level. [naghdizadeganjahromi2026adaptive] found that training without departure uncertainty performed better in an imbalance-market setting. That result is a useful foil: conservative bounds have a measurable price, and our cost-of-guarantee metric can show it.
4. **Fallback (b), the degradation-aware Pareto front, should not be added.** It adds an objective and hyperparameters, does not strengthen the guarantee story, and degradation in V2G MPC is already reported [diazlondono2026open].
5. **Fallback (c), an independent benchmark, should be partly absorbed rather than adopted.** GNN-DT and EV-GNN code is public. Running at least one of them as a baseline on our scenarios gives an independent comparison at low extra cost. A stand-alone benchmark paper has weaker novelty than the main set.

**Decision rule after R1 (and R2/R3):**
- If [orfanoudakis2026can] uses a penalty, a learned or heuristic filter, or a solver-based projection without a multi-step departure proof, proceed as recommended.
- If it proves a per-step departure and power guarantee but does not test zero-shot size transfer against an optimum, proceed. Narrow the claim to the size-agnostic transfer and the cost-of-guarantee analysis, and promote the conformal extension to a co-equal contribution.
- If it already proves the guarantee and tests size transfer, switch to fallback (a) as the main contribution. Use the projection as an established component (citing it) and make (c) the evaluation backbone.
