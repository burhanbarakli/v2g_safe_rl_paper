\section{Problem Formulation}\label{sec:problem}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Station, EVs and energy dynamics}\label{sec:station}

A CPO runs a station with $N$ chargers $\mathcal N=\{1,\dots,N\}$ behind one transformer, and each charger hosts at most one EV, whose quantities carry the index $i$ of its charger.
Time is divided into steps $t=0,\dots,T-1$ of length $\Delta t$ [h].
At every step the station observes the transformer rating $P_{\sm{tr}}(t)$, the inflexible load $P^{\sm{inf}}(t)\ge0$ and the photovoltaic (PV) generation $P^{\sm{pv}}(t)\ge0$ in kW.
Energy is bought at the price $c^{\sm{ch}}(t)$ and sold at the price $c^{\sm{dis}}(t)$ [\texteuro/kWh], and both prices may be negative.

EV $i$ arrives at step $t_i^{\sm{arr}}$ and is controlled until its departure step $d_i$, the last controlled step, so its departure energy is $E_i(d_i+1)$, with $E_i(t)$ the battery energy at the start of step $t$.
At step $t$ the EV has $\tau_i(t)=d_i-t+1$ remaining actions, and $\mathcal N(t)$ is the set of the $n(t)$ occupied chargers.
Let $p_i(t)$ [kW] be the power commanded at charger $i$, positive for charging and negative for V2G discharging, and let $\tilde p_i(t)$ be the power reported by the simulator.
With $p^{+}=\max(p,0)$ and $p^{-}=\max(-p,0)$, the battery energy evolves as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamics}
E_i(t+1)=E_i(t)+\Delta t\,g_i\big(p_i(t)\big),\qquad g_i(p)=\eta_i^{\sm c}p^{+}-p^{-}/\eta_i^{\sm d},
\end{equation}
with charge and discharge efficiencies $\eta_i^{\sm c},\eta_i^{\sm d}\in(0,1]$.
Per-step physical limits restrict the power to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limits}
\pmn_i(E)\le p_i(t)\le \bar P_i(E),\qquad \pmn_i(E)=-\min\Big\{\Pdis_i,\ \eta_i^{\sm d}\frac{(E-E_i^{\min})^{+}}{\Delta t}\Big\},\qquad E=E_i(t),
\end{equation}
where the maximum charging power $\bar P_i(E)\ge0$ includes the charging taper and the capacity limit, $\Pnom_i=\sup_E\bar P_i(E)$ and $\Pdis_i$ is the maximum discharging power.
The theory holds for the general map $g_i$, but EV2Gym reports, bills and meters the battery-side energy change as the charger power \cite{orfanoudakis2025ev2gym}.
Our experiments therefore use $\eta_i^{\sm c}=\eta_i^{\sm d}=1$, a convention that removes the round-trip loss of V2G arbitrage (\Sec{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Departure requirement and transformer limit}\label{sec:requirement}

Each EV requests a desired departure energy $E_i^{\sm{des}}$, and a station-level request pipeline turns it into the admitted requirement $\Ereq_i$.
The pipeline runs once per test replay before any controller, and every compared method and both optima receive the same admitted requirement.
At arrival it caps the request to the energy that full-power charging can reach during the stay, about $10^{-6}$ kWh below that value.
Admission control then solves a small feasibility linear program (LP) and lowers the new request if the connected EVs could otherwise not all be served under the forecast transformer limits.
Requests already admitted are never reduced.
Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cap}
\Ereq_i\le\min\big\{E_i^{\sm{des}},\ \text{the energy reachable at full power from }E_i^{\sm{arr}}\text{ during the stay}\big\},
\end{equation}
and Section~\ref{S-sec:pipeline} gives the details.
The departure requirement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:requirement}
E_i(d_i+1)\ \ge\ \Ereq_i .
\end{equation}
Without the cap, some arrivals could not be served by any controller, and a guarantee about them would be void.
For every method, shortfall is measured against the shared admitted requirement, and the energy removed by the pipeline is reported separately.

The net transformer power $P^{\sm{inf}}(t)-P^{\sm{pv}}(t)+\sum_{i}p_i(t)$ must stay within $[-P_{\sm{tr}}(t),P_{\sm{tr}}(t)]$, so the EV aggregate has the transformer limit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transformer}
-\Crev(t)\le\sum_{i\in\mathcal N}p_i(t)\le C(t),\qquad
C(t)=P_{\sm{tr}}(t)-P^{\sm{inf}}(t)+P^{\sm{pv}}(t),\qquad \Crev(t)=P_{\sm{tr}}(t)+P^{\sm{inf}}(t)-P^{\sm{pv}}(t).
\end{equation}
The forward limit $C(t)$ bounds charging and the reverse limit $\Crev(t)$ bounds V2G export.
For the current step the safety layer uses the measured load and PV, whereas the policy observes only noisy forecasts of future values.

\subsection{Online control problem and optima}\label{sec:online}

At each step the CPO chooses $p(t)\in\R^N$ using only the information available at step $t$.
The profit of a controller $\pi$ in an episode, in \texteuro, is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:objective}
J^{\pi}=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\Delta t\sum_{i\in\mathcal N(t)}\big(c^{\sm{dis}}(t)\,\tilde p_i^{-}(t)-c^{\sm{ch}}(t)\,\tilde p_i^{+}(t)\big),
\end{equation}
and the controller should maximise its expectation subject to \Eqs{eq:dynamics}{eq:limits}, \Eq{eq:requirement} for every EV and \Eq{eq:transformer} at every step.
With the weights $w_E=w_P=10$\,\texteuro/kWh for every method, the penalised profit is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:jpen}
J^{\sm{pen}}(\pi)=J^{\pi}-w_E\sum_i\Delta E_i^{\sm{sh}}-w_P\,\Delta t\sum_t\Big[\Big(\sum_i\tilde p_i(t)-C(t)\Big)^{+}+\Big(-\Crev(t)-\sum_i\tilde p_i(t)\Big)^{+}\Big],
\end{equation}
where $\Delta E_i^{\sm{sh}}=(\Ereq_i-E_i(d_i+1))^{+}$ is the departure shortfall.

Two perfect-information LPs, solved with HiGHS \cite{huangfu2018parallelizing}, know the whole episode and serve as references.
Both model EV2Gym's taper with outer linear rows, exact except on a one-step transition band and a 1\% margin (Lemma~\ref{S-lem:taperrows}), and both allow for EV2Gym's unbilled 0.01 kWh rounding gain.
The soft-penalty optimum $J^{\sm{pen,LP}}$ maximises \Eq{eq:jpen} with slack variables for shortfall and overload.
For every simulated trajectory, with shortfall measured against the shared admitted requirement, $J^{\sm{pen}}(\pi)\le J^{\sm{pen,LP}}$ (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:softoracle}), including trajectories with shortfall or overload.
The main metric is therefore well defined for every compared method, as the absolute gap in \texteuro{} per charger and episode,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
\mathrm{gap}^{\sm{pen}}(\pi)=\frac{J^{\sm{pen,LP}}-J^{\sm{pen}}(\pi)}{N}\ \ge0 .
\end{equation}
A relative gap is not used per episode, because $J^{\sm{pen,LP}}$ is often close to zero or negative, and the supplementary material reports relative gaps only as ratios of sums.
The hard optimum $J^{\sm{LP,ceil}}$ enforces the departure requirements and the transformer limit as constraints, after a first stage that minimises the total shortfall.
It bounds the profit of every controller that respects the transformer limit and leaves no more shortfall (Proposition~\ref{S-prop:oracle}), and it serves the analysis of the cost of the guarantee.

\subsection{Partially observable Markov decision process}\label{sec:mdp}

The online problem is a POMDP, because future arrivals, prices beyond a short window and future loads are not observed.
We act on the current observation with a feed-forward actor and critic.
The policy outputs one raw action $a_i(t)\in\R$ per charger, scaled to kW as $x_i(t)=P^{\sm{ref}}a_i(t)$ with $P^{\sm{ref}}=11$ kW, and the safety layer of \Sec{sec:projection} maps it to the commanded power without any squashing function.
Its reward is the realised profit of the step, with no penalty for the departure requirement or the transformer limit, because the safety layer enforces both.
For training, the reward is divided by $N r^{\sm{ref}}$ with $r^{\sm{ref}}=c^{\sm{ref}}P^{\sm{ref}}\Delta t$ and $c^{\sm{ref}}=0.25$\,\texteuro/kWh, so that returns do not grow with $N$, and all reported results use the undivided profit.
Arrivals, prices, loads and PV evolve independently of the actions, every EV departs before the episode ends, and the discount factor $\gamma=0.99$ gives an effective horizon of about one day at $\Delta t=15$ min.
