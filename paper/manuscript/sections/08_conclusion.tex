\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------

This paper combined a size-agnostic attention policy with a feasibility-preserving safety layer for V2G charging control at a CPO station.
The layer projects raw actions onto taper-aware per-EV bounds and the two-sided transformer limit in $O(N\log N)$ time, during training and deployment.
Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance} gives a two-layer result.
Under the one-step model and the grid-headroom condition, the transformer limit holds at every step for every sequence of raw actions.
Every EV whose stay contains no infeasible step meets its admitted departure requirement, and a deficit bound limits the shortfall of the others.

Regarding constraint satisfaction, \RES{one sentence on overload energy, shortfall and E4, Tables~\ref{S-tab:t3} and~\ref{S-tab:t3b}}.
The cost of the guarantee was \RES{one sentence on $\Delta J^{\sm{pen}}$ and $\Delta$ profit per EV served, \Tab{S-tab:t5}}.

For a CPO, three limitations matter most.
First, the departure result needs declared requirements and departure times, or a calibrated departure bound with marginal coverage only.
Second, the transformer result assumes that each EV draws the commanded power, and real battery management systems may draw less.
Third, the economics omit round-trip losses and battery degradation.
Future work can add measured-energy feedback to the bounds, extend the feasible set to several transformers and linearised voltage limits, and certify joint feasibility over the future.
Tests with recorded charging sessions would narrow the remaining gap between the model and practice.
