\subsection{Main comparison}\label{sec:res-main}

\Tab{tab:t1} compares all methods at the training sizes $N\in\{10,20\}$, pooled over profiles and ratings.
\method{} had a gap of 1.290 and 0.973\,\texteuro{} per charger at $N=10$ and $N=20$, against 2.204 and 1.569 for Attention+penalty and 1.792 and 1.258 for Attention+PPO-Lagrangian.
The differences to Attention+penalty were $-0.915$ $[-1.254,-0.601]$ and $-0.595$ $[-0.797,-0.412]$, with Holm-adjusted Wilcoxon $p$-values of $1.3\times10^{-5}$ and $0.0014$ and rank-biserial correlations of $-0.24$ and $-0.19$.
Their IQMs were close, 0.712 against 0.751 and 0.719 against 0.748, so the mean advantage of \method{} came mostly from a minority of episodes with large penalties.
Deep Sets+safety layer had a lower gap than \method{}, 0.984 and 0.722, with differences of 0.306 $[0.218,0.397]$ and 0.251 $[0.220,0.286]$ (Holm $p<10^{-65}$).
Attention+safety layer without departure bound reached 0.776 at both sizes, below \method{} by 0.514 $[0.372,0.679]$ and 0.198 $[0.145,0.259]$ (Holm $p<10^{-45}$).

At $N=10$, every rule and eMPC-HiGHS with the safety layer had a lower mean gap than \method{}, from 0.790 for the price-threshold rule to 1.145 for eMPC-HiGHS.
For the price-threshold rule and eMPC-HiGHS, the differences of 0.500 $[0.341,0.680]$ and 0.144 $[0.072,0.219]$ agreed with the Wilcoxon tests (Holm $p=1.1\times10^{-9}$ and $5.7\times10^{-59}$).
With the other rules, the intervals excluded zero, but rank-biserial correlations of $-0.39$ to $-0.47$ showed \method{} lower on most replays, so these comparisons are inconclusive.
MLP+safety layer also had a lower mean gap at $N=10$, 1.025 (Holm $p=0.014$).

At $N=20$, \method{} had a lower gap than LLF, sLLF, AFAP and round robin with the layer, 0.973 against 1.099 (Holm $p<10^{-24}$).
Price-threshold+safety layer and eMPC-HiGHS+safety layer stayed lower, at 0.788 and 0.846, with differences of 0.185 $[0.137,0.238]$ and 0.127 $[0.098,0.161]$ (Holm $p\le9.8\times10^{-12}$).
Without the safety layer, the price-threshold rule had gaps of 156.370 and 159.518 and eMPC-HiGHS 108.138 and 103.765, mostly from energy not served.

\subsection{Zero-shot transfer across station sizes}\label{sec:res-scaling}

\Fig{fig:zeroshot} and \Tab{S-tab:t2} show the gap against $N$ for weights trained at $N\in\{10,20\}$.
At $N=50$, 100 and 200, \method{} had gaps of 0.921, 0.881 and 0.902, and the same policy trained at $N=50$ had 0.884, 0.857 and 0.869.
The transfer gaps of \method{} to this reference were 0.037 $[0.026,0.050]$, 0.023 $[0.012,0.033]$ and 0.033 $[0.022,0.046]$.
Deep Sets+safety layer had gaps of 0.698, 0.649 and 0.675 at these sizes, below the reference by 0.186 to 0.208.
At $N=5$, outside the training sizes, the gap of \method{} rose to 2.292 against 1.663 for Deep Sets+safety layer and 0.956 for Price-threshold+safety layer.

\method{} had a higher mean gap than Price-threshold+safety layer at every size of the test grid.
The differences ranged from 0.107 $[0.065,0.151]$ at $N=100$ to 1.335 $[0.919,1.840]$ at $N=5$, with Holm $p\le3.1\times10^{-4}$.
Deep Sets+safety layer had a lower mean gap than this rule only at $N\ge20$, with 0.649 to 0.722 against 0.774 to 0.793, and a higher one at $N=5$ and $N=10$.
No test compares these two methods directly.
Attention+penalty had a gap of 0.929 at $N=200$, a difference to \method{} of $-0.027$ $[-0.060,0.016]$.
Its interval includes zero, whereas the Wilcoxon test favoured \method{} (Holm $p=2.0\times10^{-5}$, rank-biserial $-0.35$), so this comparison is inconclusive.

At the stress level $\rho_{\sm{tr}}=3$ kW, \method{} had gaps of 2.979 at $N=20$ and 1.586 at $N=200$ (\Tab{S-tab:ts2}).
Deep Sets+safety layer had 2.400 and 1.423 there, and the variant without departure bound 0.983 and 0.829.

\subsection{Constraint satisfaction}\label{sec:res-constraints}

\Fig{fig:constraints} shows energy not served, overload energy and E4 against $N$ for the main methods.
No transformer overload occurred in any of the 102,750 test episodes run through the safety layer (\Tab{S-tab:t3c}).
Without the layer, Attention+penalty had 3,730 overload steps, Attention+PPO-Lagrangian 3,274 and MLP+penalty 3,648 over all configurations.
Averaged over the 36 test-grid configurations, 6.7\%, 7.9\% and 17.0\% of their episodes had overload (\Tab{S-tab:t3}).
For every non-conformal method with the departure bound, no EV was left short in an episode without infeasible steps, consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:invariance}(b).
In \Tab{S-tab:t3b}, every fallback activation belonged to the case of excess required power, and the three cases that A6 excludes did not occur.

Learned policies with the departure bound caused infeasible steps.
Over all episodes, \method{} had 17,678 infeasible steps and 39,771 EVs whose stay contained one, and it left 6,158 of 233,016 admitted EVs unserved.
Deep Sets+safety layer had 12,122 infeasible steps, 27,949 affected EVs and 5,038 unserved EVs.
LLF with the safety layer had one infeasible step and Price-threshold+safety layer 1,675.
E4 of \method{} rose from 65.86\% at $N=5$ to 92.58\% at $N=200$, and infeasible steps per episode fell from 4.64 to 0.58.
For Deep Sets+safety layer, E4 was higher at every size, with Holm-adjusted $p\le0.0043$ up to $N=100$.
At $N=200$, its interval of $[0.9,3.6]$ percentage points excluded zero, but the Holm-adjusted $p$ was 0.94, so this size is inconclusive.
The measured shortfall of \method{} was 0.009 times the a-priori deficit bound, as a mean over configurations of the episode means for episodes with a positive deficit.

\subsection{Effect of the departure bound}\label{sec:res-bound}

The departure lower bound did not improve service for the learned attention policy.
Attention+safety layer without departure bound had a lower mean gap than \method{} at every station size (\Tab{S-tab:t2}).
Its advantage ranged from 0.117 $[0.094,0.143]$ at $N=100$ to 1.266 $[0.927,1.652]$ at $N=5$, with Holm $p\le1.3\times10^{-30}$.
The variant left 0.99\% of the admitted EVs unserved against 2.24\% for \method{}, with 0.09 against 0.43 kWh of energy not served per episode (\Tab{S-tab:t3}).
Summed over all episodes, these shares correspond to 1,551 against 6,158 unserved EVs (\Tab{S-tab:t3c}).
At the stress level and $N=20$, the variant left 2.59\% of the EVs unserved against 12.04\% (\Tab{S-tab:ts2}).
The comparison is not a clean ablation, because the variant learned from the penalty reward with its own tuned weight, whereas \method{} learned from the profit reward.

\method{} also left more EVs unserved than its penalty-trained twin without any layer, 2.24\% against 1.86\% and 6,158 against 1,451.
Its mean energy not served was lower, however, with 0.43 against 0.63 kWh per episode.
Attention+PPO-Lagrangian left 0.20\% of the admitted EVs unserved, the penalty-trained policy with the layer at deployment 0.46\% and Price-threshold+safety layer 0.44\%.
LLF, sLLF, AFAP and round robin with the layer left no EV unserved.
\method{} commanded exactly the departure lower bound on 27.8\% to 42.3\% of the occupied charger-steps, depending on $N$ (\Tab{S-tab:t3b}).

\subsection{Cost of the guarantee}\label{sec:res-cost}

\Fig{fig:cost} and \Tab{S-tab:t5} compare \method{} with its penalty-trained twin on the same episodes.
In $J^{\sm{pen}}$, Attention+penalty minus \method{} was $-3.462$ $[-4.639,-2.388]$\,\texteuro{} per admitted EV at $N=5$ and approached zero with growing $N$, to $-0.042$ $[-0.094,0.025]$ at $N=200$.
The Wilcoxon tests on the replay-level $J^{\sm{pen}}$ differences gave Holm-adjusted $p\le2\times10^{-4}$ at every size.
At $N=200$, the interval of the mean includes zero, whereas the median shift favours \method{} (Holm $p=1.7\times10^{-6}$), so the size of this difference is inconclusive.
In raw profit, the difference was positive at $N\le20$, from 0.142 $[0.075,0.218]$\,\texteuro{} per admitted EV at $N=5$ to 0.064 $[0.039,0.091]$ at $N=20$.
It was negative at $N\ge50$, down to $-0.092$ $[-0.120,-0.065]$ at $N=200$.
For the MLP pair, the $J^{\sm{pen}}$ difference was between $-6.958$ and $-1.383$\,\texteuro{} per admitted EV.

\subsection{Further results}\label{sec:res-further}

The conformal variant achieved coverages of 0.992, 0.950, 0.902 and 0.799 for targets of 0.99, 0.95, 0.90 and 0.80 (\Tab{S-tab:t7}).
Its gap was 27.341 at $\alpha=0.01$, 1.292 at $\alpha=0.05$ and 1.958 at $\alpha=0.2$, against 1.210 with known departure steps.
At $\alpha=0.01$, the re-certified requests reduced the charged energy to 791.4 kWh per episode against 1396.5 kWh with known departures.
With the safety layer added at deployment only, the penalty-trained policy had a gap of 0.930 at $N=200$ (\Tab{S-tab:t2}).
Its difference to \method{}, $-0.028$ $[-0.061,0.014]$ with Holm $p=9.9\times10^{-6}$, is also inconclusive.
One control step of \method{} took 7.76 ms at $N=200$, against 5.99 ms for Deep Sets+safety layer (\Tab{S-tab:t6}).
On EV2Gym's 25-charger configuration, \method{} and Price-threshold+safety layer both had a gap of 0.507 (\Tab{S-tab:ts3}).
